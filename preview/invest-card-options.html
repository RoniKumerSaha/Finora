Investments — nine ways to draw a contract (plus two)

The Investments screen today renders each holding as a wide row card: a type pictogram, the name, a type PILL, a line of terms (rate · term · institution), a maturity countdown, and two figures on the right — NOW (what the bank would pay today) and AT MATURITY (what you get when the term ends) — separated from the identity by a vertical rule.

Below are nine redraws, plus I10 — the bond's instrument carrying the passbook's ledger, the combination of the two you liked — and I11, the unified certificate that solves the long-DPS problem. They differ in what shape carries the money — a growth curve, a countdown ring, a ladder of installments, a passbook of deposits, a tower of blocks — because "৳12,345 now / ৳15,234 at maturity" is two numbers with no relationship drawn between them. The gap between them is the interest, and no current card shows it as a shape.

1 Two values, no drawn relationship. NOW and AT MATURITY sit as stacked text; the eye cannot see that the second is the first plus interest. A growth curve, a wedge or two pots makes the gap a shape.
2 The maturity countdown is the most time-sensitive fact on the card ("Matures in ~2.4y") and it is the smallest, greyest text in the left column.
3 For a DPS, the money arrives as a monthly contribution — the card shows a single lump figure, so the recurring rhythm that defines the scheme is invisible.
4 Every card is the same shape with different numbers. DPS, FDR and savings differ in kind — a monthly deposit scheme and a fixed deposit receipt are different objects — but the card does not say so.
