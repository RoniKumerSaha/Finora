Progress bars — one bar, one meaning

Every progress bar in Finora is the same bar: a thin rail with a rounded end, filled in the instrument's own colour, with the figure it represents printed beside it. The bar is never a percentage on its own — the headline above it says the count ("3 OF 10 PAID", "7% of the principal settled"), so the reader never has to guess the denominator.

And only an actual item gets one. A loan, a debt and a DPS are actual — money really moves against them — so a bar tracking them tracks something that actually changes. An FDR or a savings account is not: the money goes in once and then it waits, so a bar there would be a decoration pretending to be a measurement.

A bar also never appears on a planned item, and never inside an edit form. A plan is a projection, so its rail could only ever be empty; and a bar on a form invites the reader to think the number is editable — it isn't. Progress comes from transactions, not from typing. A debt's own certificate does carry the bar, because a certificate is a statement of fact, not a field to fill in.

3 · The rule — actual items only
Where a bar is allowed
Rule

Only an ACTUAL item gets a progress bar. Something is actual when money really moves against it — a loan, a debt, a DPS. A lump sum is not: it is paid once and then it waits, so it gets no bar at all.

And it never appears on a planned item, nor inside an edit form. A plan is a projection — its rail could only ever be empty, and an empty rail is noise. A form invites the reader to think the number is editable; it is not, because progress comes from transactions, not from typing. A debt's certificate on its own page is different: that is a statement of fact, so it carries the bar.
