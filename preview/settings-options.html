Settings — four directions

Settings is the one screen in the app that speaks no document language. Every other surface is a certificate, a receipt, a slip or a torn sheet; Settings is four grey cards and an About panel. That is defensible — settings usually are grey cards — but it means the screen where you erase your own data looks exactly like the screen where you change your PIN.

Each direction below keeps the real content (PIN, cloud sync, backup, wipe, version, privacy, storage count) and argues for a different idea of what Settings is. They are not variations on a card grid — they are four different screens.

A · The Control Panel
Settings is not preferences — it is the instrument panel of your own setup. You open it when something is off: the lock, the sync, the backup, the storage. So the screen is a single console of live readouts and status lamps, and every row states a condition before it offers a control. The Danger zone is the last row, in red, because on a console the red lamp is the one you look for when something is wrong.

B · The Service Ledger
Settings is a statement about this device — what it holds, what's protected, what's stored elsewhere, and what can be done to it. So it is a ruled page with a two-column ledger: the left rail names the line, the right states its figure. The wipe is not a red card; it is the final line of the statement, set in the danger ink, and the page carries a rubber stamp because a statement is a signed document.

C · The Passport
The question the user actually opens Settings to answer is "where is my money?" — and the honest answer is "on your device, and in a cloud copy under this identity, protected by this PIN." So the screen becomes an identity document: a crest and owner block on the left, the guarantees on the right, each with its own seal. Nothing here is a "setting" — every row is a fact about you.

D · The Switchboard
Every row on the current Settings screen is a task, not a preference — change the PIN, sync now, export a backup, wipe everything. So the screen becomes a board of panels: each a self-contained card with its own icon, its own status line, and a clear invitation. No card contains a paragraph and three buttons; each contains one thing. The Danger zone is the last panel, red, and the only one whose label is a verb with a cost.
